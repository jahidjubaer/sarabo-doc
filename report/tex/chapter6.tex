\section{Request Creation}
The customer picks a device and repair from the live catalogue (or lets the AI helper propose them), adds brand, model and a description, optionally up to three photos (sent only on ``Upload''), and a pickup window; the estimate shows the catalogue price range. The form checks \texttt{GET /service-availability}, and the server checks again on submission: if no approved technician in the region can do the repair at the required level, the request is refused with an explanation. Accepted requests get a tracking code and a confirmation email (Figures~\ref{fig:wt-c02} and~\ref{fig:wt-c03}).

\section{Job Portal and Offers}
A request waiting for a technician is \emph{open}: it appears on the public jobs board as a safe summary, and each matching approved technician gets one \texttt{job\_posted} notification (also when they start matching later, after approval or a new skill). Technicians see the district, pickup time, problem and photos, never the customer's name, email or street address.

An \emph{offer} has an estimate range, an inspection fee (between the minimum fee and the catalogue fee) and a note. The customer chooses one, or rejects one with a reason (price, fee or other); the job is then \emph{reposted}, the rejected technician may send one revised offer, and other matching technicians are notified. A job with no waiting offer 24 hours after its last posting goes on the admin attention list. Admins can also invite a technician to apply, or assign one directly as an override (Figures~\ref{fig:wt-t03} and~\ref{fig:wt-c06}).

\section{Offer Ranking (Smarter Matching)}
\label{sec:ranking}
Offers are ranked, not listed in arrival order. A pure scoring module (\texttt{services/offerRanking.js}) combines five factors from data Sarabo already stores, so no paid service, map API or AI is needed (Table~\ref{tab:ranking}); a technician with no history scores neutral on that factor. With two or more offers the top one gets a ``Best match'' badge, and reasons appear only when true (e.g.\ ``Rated 4.8\,$\star$ from 12 reviews'', ``In your district''). The customer can also sort by price or date, and always chooses; nothing is booked automatically.

\section{Inspection Fee and Booking}
Choosing an offer opens a Stripe Checkout for exactly its fee. When Stripe confirms the payment, one transaction records it and books the technician; if booking is no longer possible (technician busy, request cancelled, another offer chosen), the payment is refunded automatically and the customer chooses again. The fee is stored apart from the repair payment and credited toward the final price (Figures~\ref{fig:wt-c07} and~\ref{fig:wt-c10}). Table~\ref{tab:feeoutcomes} lists what happens to it when the repair does not go ahead.

Refunds take two steps: the transaction that causes the refund marks the fee and its payment row \emph{refund pending}; after it commits, Stripe is called with the idempotency key \texttt{inspection-refund-<session id>}, so at most one refund is made. A failed refund stays pending on the admin home page and can be retried with one click; it is never silently lost.

\section{Inspection, Quotation and Revision}
Only the booked technician can submit the inspection (findings and detected issues) after collecting the device, and a quote only from \texttt{inspection\_completed}. The customer sees the quote with the fee already paid and the amount due, and the button ``Approve and pay BDT \textit{X}'' leads straight to payment.

After a rejected quote the technician may revise or close the request (Figure~\ref{fig:revise}); revising archives the old quote and repeats the submit-and-decide cycle. With no action for three days the daily job closes the request, records who closed it (\texttt{technician}, \texttt{customer} or \texttt{auto}), and the inspection fee is kept and split.

\section{Payment, Repair and Receipt}
Only an approved, unpaid quote can be paid. The technician then starts the repair, posts progress updates and completes it, optionally with evidence photos; completion frees the technician and asks the customer to confirm receipt. Only that confirmation ends the request and makes the earning available, so no technician can withdraw money for a device the customer has not received.

\paragraph*{Pickup condition.} At collection the technician records the device's condition and accessories (charger, case, SIM). The customer sees the record and can dispute it, and an admin then reviews it; this protects both sides later (Figure~\ref{fig:wt-t05}).

\section{Repair Warranty}
\label{sec:warranty}
Every finished repair is covered for 30 days from confirmed receipt: the same fault coming back or a fitted part failing, but not new damage, water damage or drops (Figure~\ref{fig:warranty}).

A customer may have one open claim at a time and two per repair. Each step is a guarded database update, so none can be skipped and two people acting at once cannot both succeed. A missed deadline marks the claim overdue and lets admins take it over, and a technician with open claims cannot be removed. A refund covers the whole quote (repair payment plus credited fee) through Stripe, with one idempotency key per part; the settlement becomes \emph{reversed}, and money already withdrawn is shown as owed and deducted from future earnings (Figures~\ref{fig:wt-c16} and~\ref{fig:wt-c17}).

The wallet is computed, not stored:
\begin{align}
\text{Pending} &= \textstyle\sum \text{paid settlements not yet receipt-confirmed}\\
\text{Gross available} &= \textstyle\sum \text{receipt-confirmed settlements} + \textstyle\sum \text{kept inspection-fee shares}\\
\text{Reserved} &= \textstyle\sum \text{open withdrawal requests}\\
\text{Withdrawn} &= \textstyle\sum \text{paid withdrawals}\\
\text{Available} &= \text{Gross available} - \text{Reserved} - \text{Withdrawn}
\end{align}
Because rejection simply removes a reservation, no compensating balance write is ever needed.

\paragraph*{Final payout when a technician is removed.}
\label{sec:removalpayout}
Removing an approved technician queues their whole available balance as a bKash withdrawal in the same transaction, so both happen or neither does. The number comes, in order, from the admin's entry, an already-open request (topped up, not duplicated), the last withdrawal, or the application phone; with money owed and no valid number, the removal is refused. Pending money is not included. The payout is tagged ``final'' and sent and recorded like any withdrawal, by a manual bKash Send Money, since Sarabo has no bKash API.

\section{Cancellation Rules}
Before booking a customer can cancel freely and later ``Request again'' with the details pre-filled. After booking with a paid fee, \texttt{GET /repair-requests/:id/booking-cancellation} shows in advance whether cancelling now refunds or keeps the fee; cancelling is allowed only until the device is collected.

\section{Technician Profile Editing}
Technicians edit their own name, phone, address and service area (region and district together), but not email, national ID, approval status or availability. The area is locked during an active repair, since its pickup was booked there; after a change the technician is told about open jobs in the new area.

\section{Service-Area Map}
\label{sec:areamap}
The Service Areas page draws every district on an OpenStreetMap map (Leaflet) from the same \texttt{serviceAreas.json} that drives the request form. Sub-area coordinates were looked up once, offline, and kept only if inside their district (226 of 236). Search lists sub-areas before districts, so ``Ambarkhana'' opens at street level, not the city centre; a sub-area without a point falls back to its district's pin, and nothing is geocoded in the browser.

\section{Feedback and Moderation}
After a receipt-confirmed repair the customer may leave one review: 1--5 stars and an optional plain-text comment of up to 1,000 characters. Admins can hide or restore a review with a reason, and every change is kept in a moderation history. A customer can also file a private report about a technician (e.g.\ unprofessional behaviour, device damage, a payment issue); reports go only to admins, move through \emph{open}, \emph{under review}, \emph{resolved} or \emph{dismissed}, and are never deleted with the request. Technicians see their rating and visible reviews, never reports or reviewer identities.

\section{Admin Attention List and Reports}
\label{sec:reports}
\paragraph*{Attention list.} The admin home page puts the work that needs a person first (Figure~\ref{fig:wt-a01}): missed pickups (a technician was booked but the device was not collected), overdue pickups (the window passed with no technician), requests with no offer 24 hours after posting, stalled repairs, requests no technician in the region can take, and inspection-fee refunds still waiting on Stripe. Items are ordered by urgency, then age, and each count opens its list already filtered, for example ``Assign technicians'' with only overdue or unmatched requests.

\paragraph*{Reports page.} \texttt{GET /admin/reports?days=7|30|90} builds one read-only report for the last 7, 30 or 90 Bangladesh days (Figures~\ref{fig:wt-a08}--\ref{fig:wt-a09b}). It has seven parts:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Overview}, each figure next to the previous period of the same length: requests made, repairs finished, requests cancelled, success rate (finished $\div$ (finished + cancelled)), average days from request to finished repair, and requests turned away because no local technician offers that repair.
  \item \textbf{Trend}: requests made and repairs finished per day as a line chart, with ``Show as a table'' giving the same numbers as text.
  \item \textbf{Regions}: for each division, the requests, how many are waiting, how many of those no local technician can take, how many were turned away, and the approved and free technicians. Regions that need recruiting stand out.
  \item \textbf{Devices and repairs}: the eight most requested devices and repair types.
  \item \textbf{Pickup times}: a heat map of booked pickups by weekday and two-hour window (Friday closed), so admins can see when demand peaks.
  \item \textbf{Technician workload}: busy, free and never-assigned technicians, and the ten who finished most repairs, shown with name, region and counts only, never contact details.
  \item \textbf{Inspection fees}: fees paid and refunded, fees kept (split into the technician's 90\% and Sarabo's 10\%, and by reason: quote declined or late cancellation) and refunds still pending.
\end{itemize}
The chart colours were checked for contrast in light and dark mode. The same report is the only input to the optional AI summary (Section~\ref{sec:aisummary}), so the summary cannot mention a number the page does not show.

\section{Notifications and Reminders}
Forty-seven notification types, defined in one module (\texttt{utils/notificationEvents.js}) and each with a de-duplication key, cover offers, booking, refunds, inspection, quotes, payment, repair, returns and disputes, warranty, reviews and withdrawals. The daily job sends one ``pickup tomorrow'' reminder per request and pickup slot to the customer and the assigned technician, in the app and by email; a moved pickup is reminded again. This runs on the live site every evening at 18:00.
