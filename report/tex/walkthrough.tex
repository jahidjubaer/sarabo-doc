\chapter{System Walkthrough}
\label{ch:walkthrough}

This chapter shows the finished system through 53 figures (54 screenshots), taken on 14 October 2026 from the final code with real Firebase sign-in, real Gemini answers and Stripe in test mode. Section~\ref{sec:wt-public} shows the public pages. From Section~\ref{sec:wt-request} the screenshots follow \emph{one} repair, the cracked Samsung Galaxy A54 used throughout this report, through every state in Figure~\ref{fig:e2e}, switching between the customer's and the technician's screens as the case moves on. The last two sections show the technician's own account and the admin pages. Each picture shows the whole browser window as the user saw it, at the full width of the page. Personal email addresses are blurred, and other requests, people and figures visible in lists come from the seeded development data.

\section{Public Website}\label{sec:wt-public}
Anyone can use these pages without an account. A visitor can see what Sarabo repairs and what it usually costs, check that their area is covered, ask the AI chat a question, browse the open jobs that technicians can take, and follow a repair with its tracking code. Every page also works on a phone.

\section{Creating the Request}\label{sec:wt-request}
From here the chapter follows one repair. Rahim, a customer in Sylhet, dropped his Samsung Galaxy A54: the screen cracked, a green line appeared and the touch stopped working. He signs in, describes the problem in his own words and lets the AI fill in the form, then chooses where and when the phone should be collected.

\section{Administration}
Admins run the platform. Their overview puts the problems that need a person first; from there they manage requests and technicians, the service catalogue, reports, payouts, reviews and user roles. Most of the numbers in these screens come from the seeded development data.
